Fingerprint search

Software ranks candidates; an examiner decides

The advance

Software compares a partial print with millions of stored prints and ranks possible matches.

The check

An examiner still evaluates the ridge detail and the quality of the print.

Case · 2004

Brandon Mayfield

FBI examiners wrongly attributed a Madrid bombing fingerprint to an Oregon lawyer. He was released after about two weeks.

What the review found

Look-alikes and bias

Look-alike detail plus failures in examination and review. Examiners also discounted Spanish police disagreement.

AI and Forensics · Week 11B19 / 40

Part 3: evaluation and estimation. These tools produce statements about evidence, so the wording matters.

DNA mixtures

When one swab holds several people’s DNA

The challenge

One sample can contain small amounts of DNA from several people, overlapping in the signal.

The contribution

Probabilistic genotyping software compares possible explanations for the mixed signal.

Gets harder with
Very little DNA, degraded samples, many contributors.
Kind of AI
Mostly statistical modelling, not generative AI.
Must check
Was the lab’s validation done on samples like this one?
AI and Forensics · Week 11B20 / 40

A mature example of computational assistance. Avoid describing a mixture as cleanly separating every person.

What a likelihood ratio compares

Two explanations, one number

Explanation A

Person of interest + an unknown

The suspect and one unknown person contributed the DNA.

Explanation B

Two unknowns

Two unknown people contributed the DNA.

Common trap

The software reports a likelihood ratio of 1,000 favouring A. Does that mean 1,000-to-1 odds the suspect is guilty?

No. It says the DNA is about 1,000 times more probable under A than under B, given stated assumptions. It says nothing about guilt, and nothing about how or when the DNA got there.
AI and Forensics · Week 11B21 / 40

Confusing the likelihood ratio with the probability of guilt is a classic courtroom error (sometimes called the prosecutor’s fallacy). Keep it qualitative.

Sort it · whose DNA, and how did it get there?

Source question or activity question?

Did this DNA come from Mr. Lee?Source level: whose DNA.
Did Mr. Lee touch the knife during the attack?Activity level: DNA alone may not say.
Could the DNA have transferred via a handshake?Transfer is an activity question.
Is the profile consistent with a known sample?A source-level comparison.
When was the DNA deposited?DNA usually cannot date itself.
How many people contributed to the mixture?A question about sources in the sample.

AI and Forensics · Week 11B22 / 40

A profile may support an association with a person. DNA alone may not establish when it arrived or what that person did.

Algorithms can also correct errors

Reanalysis cuts both ways

2020 · Georgia

Kerry Robinson

A new DNA interpretation helped secure his exoneration after more than 17 years in prison.

Scientists, lawyers and an innocence organization did the work — not an autonomous computer decision.

The broader lesson

Independent reanalysis can challenge both human judgment and software output.

Transparency

In State v. Pickett (2021), a New Jersey appeals court required access to DNA software source code, under protections.

AI in forensic medicine

Promising research, hard validation

Idea
Models examine post-mortem CT scans or help organize medical-examiner records.
Gap
A result from one hospital may change on another scanner or population.
Idea
Chemical or microbial changes may help estimate how long decomposition has progressed.
Gap
Temperature, surroundings and the condition of the remains change those patterns.
Idea
AI may help estimate skeletal or dental characteristics and organize comparisons.
Limit

What a court needs to examine

Four questions before an AI result becomes evidence

Method
What scientific support exists for the method?
Application
Did the analyst use it reliably on this evidence?
Limits
What errors and unsuitable conditions are known?
Access
Can the other side meaningfully challenge the result?

In US federal courts, Rule 702 governs expert testimony; a 2023 amendment stresses that reliable application must be shown.

AI and Forensics · Week 11B38 / 40

Interactive: pick four. Strong questions target the task definition, wrong-person errors, independent validation, open-set behaviour and access for challenge. Speed and popularity tell you little about evidential reliability.

Take these into any AI claim

Four questions — and adoption is not validation

What task?

A lead, an estimate, or a statement about evidence?

What test?

Independent evidence under relevant conditions?

What limit?

Known uncertainty — and a useful way to decline?

Who can check?

Access to sources, records and meaningful review?

A product’s availability tells us little about its evidentiary reliability. Research to watch: knowing when evidence is insufficient, working across labs, improving team decisions, and letting reviewers reconstruct findings.
