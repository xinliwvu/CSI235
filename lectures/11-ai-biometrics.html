AI for Biometrics · Week 11A05 / 40

Interactive: click each card. The contrast to draw out: a biometric may only unlock something locally, or it may start a comparison against remotely stored records. Those are very different privacy and security designs.

Three recognition tasks

Same technology, three different questions

1 : 1

Verification

Does this sample match the identity I claim?

Phone unlock, passport gate.

1 : N

Identification

Which enrolled person might this be?

Searching a database for candidates.

N : N

Deduplication

Has this person enrolled before?

Avoiding duplicate registrations.

A candidate match is a probabilistic result, not proof of identity. The size of the database and the cost of an error should change how the system is run.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A06 / 40

Verification compares against one template; identification searches many; deduplication asks whether a new enrolment collides with any existing one. The gallery size matters enormously — Part 4 returns to this with arithmetic.

Sort it

Face recognition

A model compares learned facial features

How it works

A network maps a face to a template; depth and infrared sensors can add 3D shape so a flat photo is less convincing.

Still hard

Lighting, masks and glasses, ageing, twins and look-alikes.

ICCV 2025 · research

LVFace

Adapts large vision transformers to face recognition, with progressive training that stabilizes how identity features are learned. The paper reports gains on several benchmarks.

Benchmark gains still need testing on the intended people and cameras.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A14 / 40

LVFace (ICCV 2025). Emphasise the last line: a benchmark is a test set chosen by researchers; deployment needs testing on the actual population and capture conditions.

Hands

Fingerprints, palms and veins

Signal

AI helps

Sensors
Optical, capacitive and ultrasonic sensors capture different evidence.
Problems

Signal

Trade-off

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A17 / 40

Speaker recognition asks who is speaking; speech recognition asks what was said. Noise, illness, recording channels and cloned speech all complicate speaker matching.

Voice cloning changes the risk

A familiar voice is no longer proof

Three threats
Replaying a recording, fully synthetic speech, and converting one voice into another.
Testing
The ASVspoof challenge evaluates systems on spoofed versus genuine speech.
Standards
NIST SP 800-63B-4 prohibits voice biometric comparison within its digital authentication framework.
So what?
Sensitive actions need stronger authentication and a safe recovery path.
Think first, then reveal

Your “grandson” calls, upset, asking for money. What should you do?

Hang up and call back on a number you already know, or ask a question only the real person could answer. Agree a family code word in advance.
AI for Biometrics · Week 11A18 / 40

The NIST statement applies to its authentication requirements — it is not a claim that every voice application is banned. Keep the discussion defensive: never suggest a familiar voice or spoken secret is enough proof.

Clothing, bags, injury, footwear and camera angle.

MMGait combines five sensor types. EventGait explores event cameras, which record changes in brightness and cope better with low or harsh light. Ask: who should be allowed to identify people by gait in public?

Typing, touch and mouse

Behaviour as a signal

Signals
Gaps between key presses, cursor paths, swipe shapes.
Used for
Spotting unusual sessions, alongside device and transaction context.
Rule
An anomaly is a reason to investigate — not proof of fraud.
Think first, then reveal

You broke your wrist and type one-handed for six weeks. What should your bank’s system do?

Flag the change, then ask for more evidence (a passkey, a call-back) rather than lock you out. A good system expects legitimate change and offers a quick way through.
AI for Biometrics · Week 11A20 / 40

Behavioural signals change for innocent reasons: injury, a new keyboard, stress. That is why they belong in risk scoring, not as a final identity decision.

Signatures and body signals

Writing, heartbeat, pulse and brain

Signatures

Static vs dynamic

Static compares the finished signature. Dynamic also uses speed, pressure and pen motion — much harder to forge.

Caution

People vary

A natural signature changes with health, posture or the device.

ECG · PPG · EEG

Body signals

Heart electrical activity, blood-flow pulses, brain activity at the scalp.

Status

Mostly research

Identity uses remain research or specialized systems, often from wearables.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A21 / 40

ECG records the heart’s electrical activity; PPG measures optical changes linked to blood flow (the green light on a smartwatch); EEG records electrical activity at the scalp.

Specialized and emerging

Strong evidence, weak hints and long shots

Forensic
DNA and dental records support identification of people and remains (Week 11B).
Extra cues
Ear shape, lip motion, foot and footprint patterns.
Exploratory
Thermal appearance, tongue patterns, body odour — niche research.
Weak hints
Age, clothing and apparent gender are context, not identity.

A “soft” attribute can narrow a search; it should never stand in for identification.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A22 / 40

Soft biometrics like apparent age or gender are weak and error-prone. Do not infer anyone’s demographic group from appearance.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A26 / 40

Passkeys (FIDO) separate the biometric from the login: the biometric is a local unlock. This is a privacy-friendly design worth contrasting with central databases.

Airports and border gates

A faster match is not an entry decision

What the gate does

A camera compares the traveller with the photo linked to their passport (e.g. Australia’s SmartGates).

Why it helps

Fewer repeated manual checks, shorter queues.

What it needs

Staffed lanes for poor captures, children and travellers who are not eligible.

Think first, then reveal

The gate says “match”. Does that mean you may enter the country?

No. Face matching confirms you are the passport holder. Passport authenticity, eligibility and other border checks are separate decisions.
AI for Biometrics · Week 11A27 / 40

Use SmartGates as a specific official example rather than assuming every country works the same way.

Payments, retail and banking

Payments

Retail

Some stores enroll a palm or face for checkout — a central record.

Banking

Onboard

Recovery

Get back in

Needs independent evidence and an appeal path.

Cash, card or another accessible option should remain available. Enrolment, payment approval and identity matching are different roles with different risks.

Proof of personhood

Are you a unique human — without saying who you are?

The idea

Check that a person has not enrolled before, without needing their legal name.

One example

World ID uses iris-based uniqueness checks through its “Orb” device (provider’s description).

Scrutiny

Access, independent evaluation and privacy all deserve questions.

Think first, then reveal

Why might the internet want proof that an account belongs to a unique human?

To limit bots and fake accounts. But deduplication is probabilistic, and it does not prove an account will act honestly. Who controls the iris data matters as much as the matching.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A32 / 40

Part 4: what can go wrong and how good systems are designed around it.

Liveness · sort it

Presentation attack or injection attack?

A printed photo held up to the cameraSomething fake in front of the sensor.
A deepfake video fed into the app’s video streamData replaced after the camera.
A silicone fingerprint pressed on the sensorAn artifact presented to the sensor.
A replayed recording sent straight to the serverBypasses the microphone entirely.
A 3D mask worn in front of a phonePresentation attack.
A virtual camera driver pretending to be a webcamInjection: the pipeline is compromised.

A matching face can still be fake. Reliable capture needs protection across the whole pathway, not just at the camera.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A33 / 40

Presentation attack detection examines what reaches the sensor; injection attacks replace data after the sensor. Keep this at the defensive systems level.

AI on the defence

Research tools for detecting fakes and training fairly

CVPR 2026

TAR-FAS

CVPR 2026

BarbieGait

Synthetic people change clothes while keeping the same gait identity.

CVPR 2026

FlowPalm

Synthetic palms with varied geometry for rare capture conditions.

Benchmark gains still need independent testing. Synthetic data can carry unrealistic cues and inherited bias.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A34 / 40

Generative AI is on both sides: it creates better fakes and better training data and detectors. Cross-domain benchmark gains are promising, not proof of real-world robustness.

Protecting templates

You can change a password. Can you change your face?

Keep it local
Matching on the device avoids a central collection.
Encrypt
Protect templates in storage and in transit.
Research
CryptoFace (CVPR 2025) explores face matching while the data stays encrypted.
Plan the end
Revocation, retention and recovery must be designed up front.

A leaked password is reset in a minute. A leaked biometric is a permanent problem — which is why storage design matters more than matching accuracy here.

AI for Biometrics · Week 11A35 / 40

CryptoFace (CVPR 2025) explores end-to-end encrypted face recognition. The broader point: biometrics are not secrets, and they cannot be revoked like passwords.

Fairness and accessibility

Average accuracy can hide uneven failure

Hidden gaps
A good average can hide much higher error rates for some groups.
Inputs
Camera conditions and enrolment quality shape outcomes.
Test
Include affected populations and assistive technologies.
Fallbacks
Alternatives and appeals decide the real cost of a mistake.

Standard

NIST SP 800-63B-4

Sets biometric accuracy requirements across relevant demographic groups, and requires a non-biometric alternative within its scope.
