Equal treatment and fair treatment are not always the same. Whether the result is fair depends on whether the rule and its effects can be justified.

Sort it · forms of harm

Allocation, representation or interaction?

A qualified applicant is never shown to a recruiterAllocation: an opportunity is lost.
An image generator always draws nurses as womenRepresentation: a stereotype is repeated.
A voice-only app cannot be used by someone with a speech differenceInteraction: the user cannot complete the task.
A loan model approves one group less oftenAllocation: access to a resource.

A form times out before a screen-reader user can finishInteraction: usability and accommodation.

Historical data and past decisions

A model can learn yesterday’s rules

Records
Historical data describe what happened under earlier rules.
Learning
A model may learn those rules even when the organization wants change.
Scale
More records can reinforce an existing pattern, not correct it.
Think first, then reveal

A company trains a promotion model on 20 years of past promotions. Few women were promoted in that period. What will the model likely “learn”?

That the traits of people promoted in the past predict promotion — including traits linked to gender. The data are accurate about the past and still a poor guide to the future the company wants.
AI and Fairness · Part I10 / 40

NIST distinguishes systemic, computational/statistical, and human-cognitive bias.

Missing people and missing conditions

Tested in a quiet office, used on a noisy bus

Coverage

Few examples

A training set may contain few examples of the people who will use the system.

Averages

Hidden failures

Good average performance can hide failures in smaller groups.

Conditions

Real settings

Testing should match real devices, languages, accents and noise.

Think first, then reveal

Speech software was tested in quiet offices. Who will it fail first in real use?

Riders on a noisy bus, people using older phones, speakers of under-represented accents or languages. Representativeness is about conditions as well as demographics.

Labels and proxies

What could make the proxy differ from the real goal?

Proxy

Health-care spending

Stands in for: medical need

People who face barriers to care spend less while being just as sick. Spending under-counts their need.

Proxy

Past selection

Stands in for: merit

Past selection reflects past rules and past bias, not only ability.

Proxy

Library visits

Stands in for: needs study help

The student who most needs help may never come to the library.

A label is a measurement choice. Choosing it is already a decision about fairness.

Accessibility is part of fairness

Works for typical users, excludes others

The problem

A voice-only sign-in may reject a user with a speech difference.

The fix is not always the model

An alternative route with comparable access and dignity — not a humiliating detour.

Think first, then reveal

The voice system rejects a user with a speech impairment three times. What should happen next?

An accessible alternative (passkey, staff help, a text option) that is just as fast and respectful, plus a way to report the failure so the service improves.

Case study · Buolamwini & Gebru (2018)

Gender Shades: one accuracy number hid a huge gap

Darker-skinned womenhighest error among systems testedup to 34.7%
Lighter-skinned menhighest error among systems testedmax 0.8%

Three commercial gender-classification systems, tested in 2018. Errors varied sharply across combinations of skin type and binary gender labels.

Historical results: the companies later updated their systems. The lesson is about how to test.

AI and Fairness · Part I18 / 40

The authors reported errors up to 34.7% for darker-skinned females and at most 0.8% for lighter-skinned males among the systems tested.

Case study · Koenecke et al., PNAS (2020)

Speech recognition: twice the errors

Black speakersaverage word error rate, five systems35%
White speakersaverage word error rate, five systems19%

Five commercial speech-to-text systems, evaluated in 2020.

Think first, then reveal

Why does a transcript error become a fairness problem, not just a nuisance?

Because other systems — captions, job interviews, customer service, medical notes — rely on the text. An error upstream becomes an access problem downstream.
AI and Fairness · Part I19 / 40

Word error rate measures transcription edits relative to reference words.

Case study · Hofmann et al., Nature (2024)

Dialect prejudice in language models

What they tested

The same content written in African American English or in Standardized American English, given to tested language models.

What they found

Prejudice linked to the dialect — without any explicit mention of race.

Think first, then reveal

A chatbot never says anything openly racist. Does that show it is fair?

No. Polite wording does not establish fair behaviour. Bias can show up in decisions — who is recommended for a job, how someone is described — triggered by subtle cues like dialect.

Four measures, same example

Which ones say “fair”?

Question
Do groups get selected at the same rate?
Example
A: 56 of 100 = 56% · B: 44 of 100 = 44%
Verdict
Not met
Question
Among qualified people, are selection rates equal?
Example
A: 48 of 60 = 80% · B: 32 of 40 = 80%
Verdict
Met (Hardt, Price & Srebro 2016)
Question
Equal rates for qualified and for unqualified people?
Example
Qualified: 80% vs 80% · Unqualified selected: 8 of 40 = 20% vs 12 of 60 = 20%
Verdict
Met
Question
Among selected people, is the qualified share equal?
Example
A: 48 of 56 = 85.7% · B: 32 of 44 = 72.7%
Verdict
Not met

Two met, two not — on the very same decisions.

AI and Fairness · Part I25 / 40

Interactive. Try each preset button: matching one measure usually breaks another. This is the conflict on the next slide, made tangible. Ask students which measure they would defend for a hiring screen, and why.

Calibration

What does a score of 70% mean?

Calibrated

100 people in each group get a predicted success chance of 70%. About 70 succeed in each group.

But…

Calibration does not guarantee equal selection rates or equal error rates.

Think first, then reveal

In Group A, 70 of 100 people scored “70%” succeed. In Group B, only 50 of 100 do. What is wrong?

The score means different things for the two groups. A “70%” in Group B overstates the chance of success — so decisions based on it treat the groups inconsistently.

AI and Fairness · Part I27 / 40

The incompatibility result concerns specified criteria under stated conditions. It does not prove that every form of fairness is impossible.

Two more ideas

Individual and counterfactual fairness

Dwork et al.

Individual fairness

People who are similar in relevant ways should be treated similarly. The hard part: defining “relevant similarity” without an unfair proxy.

Kusner et al. (2017)

Counterfactual fairness

Would the decision change if a sensitive attribute had been different, in a specified causal model? It needs assumptions about what causes what.

Think first, then reveal

Why isn’t “flip the gender field in the spreadsheet and re-run” a real counterfactual test?

Because everything gender influenced — work history, hobbies, schooling — stays frozen. A real counterfactual asks about a different history, not a different checkbox.

A credible fairness audit

Would you trust this audit?

Think first, then reveal

An audit reports a 2-point gap between groups — from 15 people per group. What do you say?

With 15 people, a 2-point gap is within noise. Neither “fair” nor “unfair” is established. Ask for larger samples and confidence ranges.
AI and Fairness · Part I36 / 40

Suggested audit exercise: compare overall and subgroup error rates, then ask whether the subgroup samples support the claimed precision.

Sort it · appeals and recourse

Useful recourse or not?

“Correct an inaccurate qualification record, then we reconsider.”Actionable and fair.
“Increase your age.”Impossible.
“Request a human review within 5 days, by phone or online.”Usable route to reconsideration.
“The algorithm’s decision is final.”No recourse at all.
“Move to a different zip code.”Unfair and unrealistic burden.
“Here are the two factors that most affected the result.”Helpful if they are accurate and correctable.
