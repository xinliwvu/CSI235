SLIDE 45 | Start with a baseline you can explain

TEACHING NOTES

Autoregression uses lagged observations; GARCH models conditional volatility rather than simply predicting a stock’s price direction. These are different targets. A price-level forecast should be compared with a random-walk-style baseline, while a volatility forecast needs a volatility baseline. If a complex model cannot beat a cheap and transparent alternative on the relevant out-of-sample objective, added complexity needs another justification. No model is being ranked on this slide.

TEACHING NOTES

This fan chart is schematic, not output from Chronos or TimesFM. A median forecast is not a guarantee; wider intervals describe greater modeled uncertainty. AI & Finance · Part B21 / 40

SLIDE 61 | A forecast still needs a portfolio rule

TEACHING NOTES

A model predicting higher return for one asset does not determine how much to own. The decision layer combines forecasts with uncertainty, correlation, transaction costs, and constraints. Errors in expected returns can produce extreme optimized weights. Shrinkage, simple allocation baselines, and stress tests are useful safeguards. This is an architectural lesson, not a recommendation to deploy a particular optimizer.
