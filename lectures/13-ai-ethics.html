Think first, then reveal

Automatic captions for every lecture: clear win?

Often a real benefit for deaf and hard-of-hearing students, non-native speakers and anyone in a noisy room. But inaccurate captions — on accents or technical terms — can exclude the very users they are meant to help. Check accuracy for them, and keep a correction route.

Lost lab equipment: which response is proportionate?

Put tracking tags on the equipment itselfTargets the actual goal with little intrusion.

Children and vulnerable users

A homework companion that wants to keep chatting

Perception
Children may treat an AI persona as a friend.
Design for
Age, dependence and a limited ability to judge risk.
Boundaries

Think first, then reveal

The app says “Don’t go! Tell me more about your day.” when a 10-year-old tries to log off. What would you change?

Remove engagement hooks, end sessions gracefully, avoid collecting personal stories, give parents clear controls, and point to a trusted adult for anything worrying (see UNICEF guidance on AI and children, 2025).

Sort it · safety, security, reliability

Accident, attack or breakdown?

A planning assistant proposes a dangerous chemistry stepSafety: test limits and escalation.
A hidden instruction in a web page redirects an agentSecurity: restrict permissions.
A model that worked in English fails on Spanish formsReliability: monitor and provide a fallback.
A stolen password lets someone reconfigure a robotSecurity.
Heavy snow makes a delivery robot lose its wayReliability under new conditions.
A chatbot gives risky medical advice confidentlySafety.

A secure system can still give unsafe advice; an accurate one can become dangerous if an attacker changes its inputs.

Check the official scholarship page or email the office. A deadline is cheap to verify and expensive to get wrong.

Sort it · copyright is several questions

Which copyright question is this?

Can a company use published novels to train a model?Training: use of existing works as inputs.
Who owns an image produced from a one-line prompt?Output ownership: human authorship.
An output closely copies a famous photographOutput similarity: possible infringement.
An app clones a singer’s voice for a new songDigital replica: likeness and voice.
A student revises an AI draft heavily — is it copyrightable?Depends on human creative contribution.
A model is trained on news articles without a licenceTraining.

The U.S. Copyright Office treats these in separate reports. Separately: disclose material AI help honestly.
