This is not an online course. Attendance and participation are required. Attendance is taken at every meeting by QR code, so bring a phone; each unexcused absence costs one point of your participation grade.

The weekly rhythm

The week has a fixed shape, and knowing it in advance is most of what you need to succeed here.

Before class

One short warmup

A news article, a press release, or a video clip — nothing over about twenty minutes. Highly recommended. A five-question concept quiz opens the first meeting of most weeks.

Tuesday

The story

What is the problem? Told properly, with the concept built out of it. This is the closest thing to lecture the course contains.

Thursday

The activity

How can AI solve it? Every week has one, none requires a computer, and all of them show AI at work in a different field.

After class

A short reflection

Your own thinking applied to the week's story, due before the Tuesday meeting of the following week. Starts in Week 2.

Learning objectives

By the end of the course you will:

Connect AI to your fieldBasic knowledge of how AI meets neuroscience, physics, medicine, law, climate, and the arts.
See the whole landscapeExposure to foundational and use-inspired AI topics at a high level, without the programming.
Work with the toolsPractical skill with ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini for real problem-solving tasks.
Be ready for what's nextPrepared to take CSI-435, Introduction to AI, if you want to go further.

Policies

Students with disabilities

Reasonable accommodations are provided for documented physical, sensory, systemic, cognitive, learning and psychiatric disabilities. Contact the Disability Resource Center (Campus Center 137, 442-5490), which will verify the disability and recommend accommodations. Reasonable Accommodation Policy

Academic integrity

Every student is responsible for knowing the University's standards. Claims of ignorance, unintentional error, or academic pressure are not excuses. Plagiarism and other acts of academic dishonesty are punished. Undergraduate Bulletin · Graduate Bulletin

Responsible computing

Students must read and apply the University at Albany Policy for the Responsible Use of Information Technology to all electronic communication in this course.

Attendance and lateness

Attend every class and arrive on time; do not enter late or leave early without approval. Absences approved by the instructor beforehand are excused. Medical excuse policy

Incompletes

An incomplete is given only when circumstances beyond your control leave a substantial amount of work unfinished at the end of the semester. Make every effort to prevent this; the instructor is the sole judge of whether one is warranted.
